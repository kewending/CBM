# Clinical Concepts for Dementia Detection

This document defines the 10 clinical linguistic concepts used by the Concept Bottleneck Model (CBM) to detect and explain signs of dementia from the Cookie Theft picture description transcripts.

## 1. Noun-to-Pronoun Ratio (Anomia / Word-Finding Difficulties)
Patients frequently substitute specific nouns with low-effort deictic or closed-class pronouns (e.g., "this," "that," "he," "she") to bypass lexical retrieval failures.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "He is giving that to her over there."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The boy is handing a cookie to the girl."

## 2. Semantic Specificity (Generic vs. Content Words)
This tracks the degradation of semantic networks, where patients lose precise descriptors and rely heavily on generic superordinate nouns and verbs (e.g., "thing", "stuff", "doing") rather than specific content words.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "The person is getting the thing you sit on to do something with the stuff."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The boy climbed on the stool to reach the cookie jar."

## 3. Semantic Paraphasias
This refers to the use of incorrect words that are semantically related to the intended target word, often occurring when the correct word cannot be retrieved.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "The mother is washing dishes in the oven and water is falling on the floor."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The mother is washing dishes at the sink and water is spilling over."

## 4. Lexical Frequency Shifts / Impoverished Vocabulary
As access to complex vocabulary deteriorates, individuals rely on highly familiar, high-frequency, but less informative vocabulary, showing a contracted active mental lexicon.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "Somebody is doing something, and well, somebody is drying dishes."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "A mischievous brother and sister are attempting to sneak treats behind their mother's back."

## 5. Syntactic Simplification / Grammatical Constituent Density
This measures the structural intricacy of sentences. People with dementia often use simpler, shorter sentences and avoid complex grammatical constructions like subordinate clauses.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "I see a tad bit. Someone's standing on a stool. The water is falling."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The boy, who is balancing precariously on a wobbly stool, is handing a cookie to his sister."

## 6. Mean Length of Utterance (MLU) & False Starts
Simplified grammatical planning often leads to a lower MLU or an increase in abandoned syntactic clauses, stutters, and sentence fragments.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "The boy is... In her kitch-kitchen I suppose."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The children are in the kitchen with their mother."

## 7. Silent and Filled Pause Rate
The frequency of vocalized fillers (e.g., "uh", "um") or explicitly transcribed silent pauses (e.g., [pause]) points to severe lexical search blocks or motor-planning delays.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "The um um boy is [pause] reaching up there."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The boy is reaching into the jar."

## 8. Repetition Rate and Speech Repairs
This concept measures perseverative repetitions of identical words or phrases and speech repairs (abandoned segments followed by corrections), indicating episodic working memory deficits.
* **Positive Sample (High presence):** "The the the boy is falling off the the stool."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The boy is falling off the stool."

## 9. Information Content Units (ICU) / Propositional Idea Density
This quantifies communicative efficiency by looking at the ratio of meaningful propositions (target visual concepts like "sink", "stool", "cookie") to the total word count. Patients often produce "empty speech," where word count is high but semantic information is low.
* **Positive Sample (Low ICU density):** "No I don't see anything else going on over here, just that one thing right there."
* **Negative Sample (High ICU density):** "The mother is washing dishes and the water is spilling out of the sink."

## 10. Global Semantic Coherence / Discourse Impairment
This evaluates the ability to maintain an overall narrative theme. A reduction in coherence reveals semantic drift, tangentiality, rambling speech patterns, and disjointed narrative connections.
* **Positive Sample (High drift):** "I used to have a kitchen window like that. We lived in a house with a big yard. My son always liked cookies."
* **Negative Sample (Normal):** "The children are trying to steal cookies from the jar while their mother is distracted by the overflowing sink."
